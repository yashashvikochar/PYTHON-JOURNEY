Aircraft telemetry includes hundreds of parameters grouped into five core categories.
Here is what comes in an aircraft telemetry data stream:
1. Flight Dynamics and Navigation
Altitude: Barometric height, radar height, and GPS altitude.
Airspeed: Indicated airspeed, true airspeed, and Mach number.
Attitude: Pitch, roll, and yaw angles of the aircraft.
Position: Exact GPS coordinates and heading.
Rates of Change: Vertical speed and turn coordination rates.

2. Propulsion and Engine Health
RPM: Fan, compressor, and turbine rotational speeds.
Temperatures: Exhaust gas temperature (EGT) and inter-stage turbine temperature (ITT).
Pressures: Engine oil pressure, fuel pressure, and hydraulic pressure.
Fuel Metrics: Fuel flow rate, total fuel remaining, and fuel tank temperatures.
Vibration: Engine casing vibration levels to detect internal wear.

3. Aircraft Systems Status
Electrical: Bus voltages, battery charge levels, and generator loads.
Hydraulics: Fluid levels, pressures, and valve positions for landing gear and brakes.
Environmental: Cabin pressure, cockpit temperature, and oxygen supply levels.Actuators: 
Deflection angles of ailerons, elevators, rudders, and flaps.
Landing Gear: Weight-on-wheels sensor status and gear door locks.

4. Structural and Environmental Conditions
G-Force: Normal, longitudinal, and lateral acceleration forces.
Structural Strain: Stress measurements on the wings, fuselage, and tail via strain gauges.
External Environment: Outside air temperature (OAT), static pressure, and icing indicators.

5. Pilot Inputs and Automation
Control Column: Physical positions of the yoke, sidestick, and rudder pedals.
Throttle Position: Throttle lever angle (TLA) commanded by the pilot.
Autopilot Status: Engagement modes, flight director cues, and automated targets.
